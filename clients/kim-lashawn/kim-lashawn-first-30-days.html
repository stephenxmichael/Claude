05·Your style, every week

Show your taste, every week

Your outfits, accessories, and genuine finds show people your taste. Over time, they teach your audience what they can trust you to recommend.

From your TikTok and Instagram. Sunglasses, hoops, gold chains, and statement bags are already part of your look.

How style shows up
  • Weeks 1 and 3: a dedicated Friday style video.
  • Weeks 2 and 4: a short style moment inside Wednesday’s lifestyle vlog.
  • Every week: an optional Thursday post for an outfit, accessory, or genuine find.
Capture it where you already are
Before workBefore PilatesBefore dinnerBefore an outing

A style moment takes a minute by the mirror or the door. It fits into your day; it doesn’t need its own production day.

Short on time? A photo carousel of the outfit or the find works for your optional Thursday post.

09·Women’s safety education

Approachable, responsible, and yours

Your firearm instructor background is an important part of your expertise. Your Houston business is still being established, so this month is about introduction, not promotion.

The goal: help women get to know your perspective and your teaching approach, address beginner concerns, and make the subject feel approachable.

This month

Talk to the camera

Your story, your perspective, and the questions beginners have are enough. Class footage can become content once your Houston business is operating.

Topics to grow into
  1. 01Your instructor story and your qualifications, stated accurately
  2. 02Common concerns beginners have
  3. 03Misconceptions about women and firearm education
  4. 04What responsible ownership involves, at a high level
  5. 05Why safe storage and ongoing education matter
  6. 06An introduction to firearm care, from you as a qualified instructor
Invitations that fit right now

“What would you want to learn first?”

“Would you like updates when I start offering classes in Houston?”

Invite interest in what’s coming, without suggesting classes are open for booking yet.

Keep it educational
  • These are content topics, not technical or weapon-handling instruction.
  • Plan and verify any future care demonstration yourself, using the manufacturer’s guidance.
  • Check each platform’s current rules on firearm-related content before posting.

Kim LaShawn · Your First 30 Days of ContentFilming and editing
10·Filming and editing made simple

Keep it focused. Keep it you.

Mondays and safety Fridays

Camera talks

  • Begin with your main point.

    Skip the long introduction. Your first sentence is the reason to keep watching.

  • One idea per video.

    If you have a second idea, save it for another week.

  • Sound like yourself.

    You’re already comfortable on camera. Editing should tighten you, not change you.

  • Test 30 to 60 seconds.

    Go longer when the story genuinely needs it.

Wednesdays

Mini vlogs

  • A beginning.

    Set up the moment in a sentence: where you are, or what you almost didn’t do.

  • An experience.

    Short clips of the activity itself.

  • A reflection.

    One honest thought at the end that ties it back to this chapter.

  • Three kinds of shots.

    A wider view, a detail, and you taking part.

Trim the extras

Cut repetition, long pauses, and unnecessary setup.

Caption clearly

Readable captions, placed where they don’t cover your face.

Keep music low

Your voice should always be easy to hear.

Record vertically

Hold your phone upright for every clip.

Wipe your lens

A quick clean before you film makes a visible difference.

Face your light

Use window light or your own light, in front of you.

12·A realistic weekly workflow

Made to fit a full-time life

  1. The weekend
    About 45 to 60 minutes
    One filming session

    Record Monday’s camera talk and Friday’s video back to back, while your setup is already in place.

  2. During the week
    A few minutes at a time
    Capture as you live

    Film short clips during something you already planned: Pilates, an outing, an evening at home. Grab your style moment on the way out the door.

  3. One evening
    A short session
    Edit your vlog

    Assemble Wednesday’s clips, trim them, and add captions, separate from your filming day.

  4. Always
    Your backup plan
    Save your extras

    Keep leftover clips and outfit photos in an album for optional posts, or for a busy week when filming isn’t possible.

Work smarter

One outing, three posts

Before you leave
An outfit post

Show the look you chose and why.

While you’re out
A lifestyle vlog

Wide, detail, and you, enjoying it.

Afterward
A reflection

One thought the day gave you.

Kim LaShawn · Your First 30 Days of ContentTrust before monetization
13·Build trust before monetization

Recommendations start with trust

People buy what you recommend when they already believe you. That belief comes from watching you genuinely use and enjoy what you share, which is why style shows up every week.

  1. 1
    Show what you use

    Clothing, accessories, and everyday products that are really part of your life.

  2. 2
    Let it appear naturally

    In your outfits, routines, and vlogs, without a sales pitch.

  3. 3
    Notice the questions

    “Where’s that from?” is a signal. Keep a running list of what people ask about.

  4. 4
    Recommend, when it fits

    Later, through TikTok Shop or other ways, for items your audience already trusts you on.

Your milestone
1,000
TikTok followers

Your short-term goal is 1,000 TikTok followers, then applying for TikTok Shop creator access once your account is eligible. TikTok sets and changes the requirements, so we’ll confirm what’s current before you apply. Reaching the milestone doesn’t guarantee approval, income, or a timeline.

About TikTok Shop

It’s a future opportunity, not a guaranteed income. Until then, every genuine recommendation builds the trust it depends on.

About replacing your income

That’s a longer-term business goal. It comes from consistent revenue over time, not from reaching a particular follower count.
