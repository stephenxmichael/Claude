07 · Women’s safety education
Shooting Stars · Content StrategyKim LaShawn · Month One
07
07 Introducing women’s safety education

Approachable. Responsible. Yours.

Your Houston instruction business is still taking shape, so this month is about introduction, not promotion.

The goal is simple: help women see you as someone they can learn from, and make the subject feel approachable, with confidence and responsible learning at the center.

This month

Talk to the camera

Your story, your perspective, and the questions beginners have are enough. Classes and classroom footage can become content once your Houston business is up and running.

Topics to grow into
  1. 01Your instructor story and your qualifications, stated accurately
  2. 02Common concerns beginners have
  3. 03Misconceptions about women and firearm education
  4. 04What responsible ownership involves, at a high level
  5. 05Why safe storage and ongoing education matter
  6. 06An introduction to care and cleaning, from you as a qualified instructor
Invitations that fit right now

“What would you want to learn first?”

“Would you like updates when I start offering classes in Houston?”

Invite interest in what’s coming, without suggesting classes are open for booking yet.

Keep it responsible
  • Keep the focus on confidence and education, not tactics or demonstrations.
  • Plan and verify any future care or cleaning demo yourself, using the manufacturer’s guidance.
  • Check each platform’s current rules on firearm-related content before posting.

08 · Filming and editing
Shooting Stars · Content StrategyKim LaShawn · Month One
08
08 Filming and editing made simple

Keep it focused. Keep it you.

Mondays & safety Fridays

Camera talks

Begin with your main point.

Skip the long introduction. Your first sentence is the reason to keep watching.

One idea per video.

If you have a second idea, save it for another week.

Sound like yourself.

You’re already comfortable on camera. Editing should tighten you, not change you.

Test 30–60 seconds.

Go longer when the story genuinely needs it.

Wednesdays

Mini vlogs

A beginning.

Set up the moment in a sentence: where you are, or what you almost didn’t do.

An experience.

Short clips of the activity itself.

A reflection.

One honest thought at the end that ties it back to this chapter.

Three kinds of shots.

A wider view, a detail, and you taking part.

Trim the extras

Cut repetition, long pauses, and unnecessary setup.

Caption clearly

Readable captions, placed where they don’t cover your face.

Keep music low

Your voice should always be easy to hear.

Record vertically

Hold your phone upright for every clip.

Wipe your lens

A quick clean before you film makes a visible difference.

Face your light

Use window light or your own light, in front of you.

09 One vlog, start to finish

The Pilates you almost skipped

Here’s how a single after-work Pilates class becomes a complete mini vlog, in three short beats.

1Beginning

Almost skipping

“I almost talked myself out of Pilates today.”

One quick clip after work. Be honest about the tired part.

2Experience

Choosing to go

“But I went anyway.”

Arriving, a detail or two, and a few seconds of you in class, filmed with the studio’s permission.

3Reflection

How it felt after

“Here’s why I’m glad I came.”

One short clip afterward, in your own words. That’s your ending.

Your shot recipe

Wide

The studio, the street, or the room you’re in.

Detail

Your mat, your shoes, your water bottle.

You

Taking part, not just narrating.

Your setup today

Your phone and the light you already own are enough to start. Film near a window when you can.

A microphone

11 · Trust before monetization
Shooting Stars · Content StrategyKim LaShawn · Month One
11
11 Build trust before monetization

Recommendations start with trust

People buy what you recommend when they already believe you. That belief comes from watching you genuinely use and enjoy what you share.

  1. 1

    Show what you use

    Clothing, accessories, and everyday products that are really part of your life.

  2. 2

    Let it appear naturally

    In your outfits, routines, and vlogs, without a sales pitch.

  3. 3

    Notice the questions

    “Where’s that from?” is a signal. Keep a running list of what people ask about.

  4. 4

    Recommend, when it fits

    Later, through TikTok Shop or other ways, for items your audience already trusts you on.

About TikTok Shop

It’s a future opportunity, not a guaranteed income. Platform requirements can change, so we’ll check what’s current when you’re ready.

About replacing your income

That’s a longer-term business goal. It comes from consistent revenue over time, not from reaching a particular follower count.

For now, build a community that trusts your taste.

12 · Your first-month review
Shooting Stars · Content StrategyKim LaShawn · Month One
12
12 Your first-month review

Look back before you plan ahead

At the end of the month, work through these questions and tick each one once you’ve answered it.

  • Did you consistently publish the three core posts?

    Consistency matters more than any single video.

  • Which videos brought new followers?

  • Which prompted meaningful comments and shares?

  • Which generated style or product questions?

    These point toward future recommendations.

  • Which sparked interest in future safety education?

  • Which formats felt easiest and most enjoyable to make?

    What you can sustain is just as important as what performs.

Then, repeat what worked.

Keep and improve your strongest formats. Months two and three will be shaped by what resonated and what you can sustain, so we’ll plan them together once this review is done.
