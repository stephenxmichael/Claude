06 Your signature series

Rediscovering her

This series isn’t “watch me recover from divorce.” It’s “I’m rediscovering myself in a new chapter, and maybe you are too.”

That gives you room for reflection, fun, Houston, work-from-home days, beauty and style, and everyday life, without the series ever feeling heavy. Your first video on this is the pilot; everything else builds out from it.

Episode 1 · The opener

“I knew who I was as a wife, but after my divorce, I realized I had to rediscover who I was as a woman.”

Then expand: “I don’t think you have to go through a divorce to understand that. Sometimes life changes and you realize you’ve spent so much time being somebody’s wife, mom, employee, caretaker, that you haven’t stopped to ask, ‘Who am I now? What do I actually like?’ So that’s what this season is for me. Rediscovering her.”

Maybe you’re rediscovering her too.

The episodes
Already in your Month 1 plan
Episode 16 · The heart of the series

I don’t want to become the old me again

You’re not trying to find the woman you were at 25. You’re meeting the woman you are now.

“People always say they want to get back to who they were before the marriage, before the kids, before life happened. But I don’t think I want to go backwards. That woman hadn’t lived what I’ve lived yet. I’m not trying to find the old me. I’m getting to know the woman I’ve become.”

Keep it fun

“I’m rediscovering what I like, so today we’re trying…”

A new Houston restaurantA dance classPilatesA new hairstyleA makeup lookA cooking classA farmers marketA new recipeA concertA museumA new neighborhoodA perfumeA trend you thought wasn’t for youHosting girlfriendsDecorating your placeA solo lunch

20 to 45 seconds with a little voiceover. This is what makes the series bingeable: people aren’t just hearing about rediscovery, they’re watching you do it.

Hooks to rotate

No need to say “Episode 17” every time. Rotate these instead:

  • “In today’s episode of rediscovering myself…”
  • “Apparently I’m learning new things about myself at this big age.”
  • “Something I’ve been realizing in this season…”
  • “I haven’t done this in years, so we’re doing it today.”
  • “I’m new to Houston, and part of making this place home is finding my places.”
  • “Question for the women who are also in a new chapter…”
  • “Maybe I’m not the only woman who needed to hear this…”

The last two matter most: you’re speaking with women, not at them.

10 Introducing women’s safety education

Approachable. Responsible. Yours.

Your firearm instructor background is an important part of your expertise. Your Houston business is still being established, so this month is about introduction, not promotion.

The goal: help women get to know your perspective and your teaching approach, address beginner concerns, and make the subject feel approachable.

This month

Talk to the camera

Your story, your perspective, and the questions beginners have are enough. Class footage can become content once your Houston business is operating.

Topics to grow into
  1. 01Your instructor story and your qualifications, stated accurately
  2. 02Common concerns beginners have
  3. 03Misconceptions about women and firearm education
  4. 04What responsible ownership involves, at a high level
  5. 05Why safe storage and ongoing education matter
  6. 06An introduction to firearm care, from you as a qualified instructor
Invitations that fit right now

“What would you want to learn first?”

“Would you like updates when I start offering classes in Houston?”

Invite interest in what’s coming, without suggesting classes are open for booking yet.

Keep it educational
  • These are content topics, not technical or weapon-handling instruction.
  • Plan and verify any future care demonstration yourself, using the manufacturer’s guidance.
  • Check each platform’s current rules on firearm-related content before posting.
11
11 Filming and editing made simple

Keep it focused. Keep it you.

Mondays & safety Fridays

Camera talks

Begin with your main point.

Skip the long introduction. Your first sentence is the reason to keep watching.

One idea per video.

If you have a second idea, save it for another week.

Sound like yourself.

You’re already comfortable on camera. Editing should tighten you, not change you.

Test 30–60 seconds.

Go longer when the story genuinely needs it.

Wednesdays & series experiences

Mini vlogs

A beginning.

Set up the moment in a sentence: where you are, or what you almost didn’t do.

An experience.

Short clips of the activity itself. Let it carry the video.

A reflection.

One honest thought at the end that ties it back to this chapter.

Three kinds of shots.

A wider view, a detail, and you taking part.

Trim the extras

Cut repetition, long pauses, and unnecessary setup.

Caption clearly

Readable captions, placed where they don’t cover your face.

Keep music low

Your voice should always be easy to hear.

Record vertically

Hold your phone upright for every clip.

Wipe your lens

A quick clean before you film makes a visible difference.

Face your light

Use window light or your own light, in front of you.

14
14 Build trust before monetization

Recommendations start with trust

People buy what you recommend when they already believe you. That belief comes from watching you genuinely use and enjoy what you share, which is why style shows up every week.

  1. 1

    Show what you use

    Clothing, accessories, and everyday products that are really part of your life.

  2. 2

    Let it appear naturally

    In your outfits, routines, and vlogs, without a sales pitch.

  3. 3

    Notice the questions

    “Where’s that from?” is a signal. Keep a running list of what people ask about.

  4. 4

    Recommend, when it fits

    Later, through TikTok Shop or other ways, for items your audience already trusts you on.
