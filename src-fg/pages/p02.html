Stop Treating It
Like A Phone

I’ve been shooting with dedicated cameras since 2014. For a long time, there was a clear gap between what I could create with my camera and what I could create with my phone.

That gap is still there.

But it’s a lot smaller than it used to be.

Today’s iPhone gives me access to tools and controls that used to keep those two worlds much further apart. More importantly, I can apply the same principles I’ve learned behind a camera to something that’s already in my pocket.

That changed the way I look at it.

“Cinematic” Is
Just A Series
Of Decisions.
M02.2
Photo · Nuance iPhone beside the pro camera 236 × 144 · both in one kit

4K doesn’t make an image cinematic. Neither does 24 fps, Log, a LUT or downloading a manual camera app.

Those tools can give you more control, but the image still depends on the decisions you make before and after you press record.

The technology gives you the tools.
You still have to make the image.
The Decisions
01Where is the light coming from?
02How is the frame composed?
03Why is the camera moving?
04What shot comes next?
05What does the color need to feel like?
The Gap Is Closing.
The Ceiling Isn’t The Same.
My professional camera still gives me things the iPhone can’t fully replicate — interchangeable lenses, natural optical depth, more flexibility in demanding shooting environments and files I can push further in post. I’m not trying to replace that. I’m trying to use the right camera for the job.
Less Friction.
More Creating.
A full camera setup can give me the image I want, but it also takes more time to build, shoot and break down. My iPhone removes a lot of that friction. If I can get close to the look I want, create faster and still deliver an image I’m proud of, that’s a trade I’ll make.
“I’ll take that gap if I can
scale my content faster.”
— Stephen
Your phone doesn’t need to replace your camera.
You just need to stop treating it like it isn’t one.
