# Field Guide 001 — Pass 02: three visual directions

Six pages. Three directions, each a cover plus the **Shoot With Intention**
interior, which is the right test page because six frames show what happens to
a layout once real photography lands in it.

Build: `node build.mjs field` → `dist/field-guide-directions.html`

---

## What changed from Pass 01

**Product identity.** "The iPhone Creator Guide" is gone. Every page now carries
**Your iPhone Is A Cinema Camera**, *Shooting Stars Field Guide 001*, with the
subtitle "A practical guide to shooting cinematic content with the camera
already in your pocket."

**Colour hierarchy — the main revision.** Pass 01 kept the Framework's
relationship and only changed the furniture, which is why it read as another
edition. Field Guides now invert it:

| | Content Framework | Field Guides |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | black / white | **cobalt** |
| Secondary | cobalt accent | **black**, technical modules |
| Ground | bone | **white**, editorial air |
| Spine | cobalt on dark, ink on light | **cobalt on light, ink on cobalt** |

No new colours. The palette values are identical to the Framework's; only their
roles moved. That spine inversion alone is the fastest tell between the two
products at a glance.

**Page count.** Furniture now reads `07 / 11`, against the 11-page architecture
in the brief. The IPHONE OVER CAMERA page is accounted for but not designed.

---

## What was preserved

The six-frame WIDE → MEDIUM → CLOSE → DETAIL → MOVEMENT → REVEAL structure, the
editorial grid, technical metadata, page numbering, QR modules, labelled
placeholders, light/dark rhythm, the annotation system, and the working copy
worth keeping ("thinking in shots, not clips").

---

## The placeholder now briefs the shoot

Cover plates carry **shoot zones**: a dashed region for the subject, a crosshair
for the phone, and a dotted keep-clear region marking the negative space the
title needs. Plus a black caption module with crop, orientation and direction.

The point is that Stephen can hold the prototype up on the day and frame against
it, rather than shooting something that later has to be cropped to fit.

Every interior frame is previewed as a **dark plate**, because real photography
is dark. It keeps the three directions comparable on layout and colour instead
of on how a placeholder happens to be tinted.

---

## Direction A — Cobalt Field Guide

Cobalt is the publication. The cover is a full cobalt field with the title at
114px in white and the photograph set *into* the colour as an inset plate, so
the colour reads as the substrate rather than a background swap.

The interior establishes a three-part page architecture: **cobalt header band /
white body / black technical footer**.

- **Strongest at:** recognition across a room; series identity.
- **Costs:** the cover leans on type, not the photograph. If 002 and 003 are
  also full cobalt, the series needs a second differentiator between issues.

## Direction B — Cinematic Photography

The photograph is the cover, full bleed, top to bottom. Cobalt arrives as an
asymmetric panel over the lower left that the image deliberately runs past.
Typography is restrained and layered on.

The interior descends **one frame → two → three** as coverage tightens, so the
grid itself carries the teaching rather than just holding it.

- **Strongest at:** premium feel; showing that photography is the product.
- **Costs:** it depends entirely on the hero image. A merely good photograph
  makes this cover ordinary, and every future issue needs one that strong.

## Direction C — Technical Field Manual

The 34px spine widens into a **160px cobalt data rail** running the full height,
carrying the publication's running technical record. On the interior it carries
the coverage index and the QR, which frees the sheet to be frames. No HUD: the
rail does that job.

Frames sit in a uniform 2 × 3 contact sheet with frame numbers and IDs. Capture
data below the cover plate is left as `—` with `[technical copy to verify]`,
rather than inventing an exposure for a photograph that does not exist yet.

- **Strongest at:** distance from the Content Framework; information design;
  the thing you actually keep beside a camera.
- **Costs:** the cover has less emotional pull than A or B, and the register
  would go clinical if the technical language were pushed any further.

---

## Recommendation

**Direction C, with A's cover scale.**

The rail is the answer to repeatability, which is the criterion that decides a
*system* rather than a single cover: Field Guide 002 and 003 inherit the rail and
change only the data in it, the way a series gets a recognisable spine. It is
also the furthest from the Content Framework, so the two products cannot be
confused, and it is the only one of the three genuinely designed to be
referenced mid-shoot.

Its one weakness is the cover, which is quieter than A's. That is fixable
without touching the system: raise the title toward A's scale and let it run
into the rail. B's descending grid is worth keeping regardless of the direction
chosen — it is the best of the three interiors, and it would drop into C.

If the priority is instead *immediate shelf recognition*, take A and borrow C's
rail for interior pages.

## Copy status

Working copy, per §13. Where Pass 01 asserted more certainty than it had, the
claim is gone rather than softened: no HDR verdict, no "faster looks choppy,
slower turns to mush", no universal Apple Log exposure rule, no cage-cooling
claim. The page-02 ranking graphic is not in this pass; when it returns it
becomes interacting variables, not a scored hierarchy.
